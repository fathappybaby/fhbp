A Warm Welcome

And we heard you, Canada. You didn't like our romcom. We got it. That's why in our next film we immediately turned up the blood and had a mother rip off her own head for no apparent reason.

And to our Brazilian contingent, you are beautiful, and we don't mean on the inside. Thank you for not B-I-T-C-H-I-N-G, like those up North.

And to our new visitors, welcome to the indie-filmmaking revolution. We are Fat Happy Baby Productions and we produce short films across genres in comedy, horror, action, and whatever our movie titled 'Rubber Head' was about. Watch them, let us know if you like them, join our community and help us make them.

Standards

Every FHBP film must include:

  1. A dog takes a dump on camera.
  2. A child actor goes diva.
  3. A limb gets ripped off (heads ARE limbs, Canada).
  4. Probably the best green screen work in town.
  5. Multiple fold-out tables (we are big on tables).
  6. Backdrop sheets, over everything (lots of steaming).

Secret Weapon

Behind every FHB short is Sean Dog, our writer, producer, and webdev. Strategically kept firmly off-camera, he is full-stop our core talent and greatest strength.

Subscriber's Benefits

There is a stretch of time between the day we lock a film and the day we beg people to suffer through it. Give us your email and we will grant you and anyone else who gave us their email exclusive access to be the first to suffer.

Concerned Writer (Second Letter)

I am writing a second time about content on your website.

My first letter, regarding Pumkin, went two weeks without a reply, which I will note here for the record. Since then my son Jacob (14) found your film Rubbber Head and has drawn the clown behind the glass eleven times in his notebook. My husband is now also having bad dreams.

I am not asking you to remove the film. I am asking you to consider a content advisory.

Deborah Lantz, mother of three
Grand Rapids, MI
via email

FHBP

We got your letter for My Little Pumpkin and put it in a file titled REDICULOUS. Please send your son's clown pictures. We like to collect evidence that people are still having creative thoughts.

FHBP has nothing to say about your husband's wet dreams

Letter 2

Concerned Writer (Big Winner With Ideas)

As someone who has actually made the 48 Hr Film Festival finals TWICE ("Last Light at Echo Park," 2023, and "Still Burn," 2025) I feel compelled to point out a fundamental misunderstanding in your approach to the format.

The 48 Hr finals reward narrative scaffolding. They reward a protagonist with an arc. They reward intentionality. Junk Haulers is competent, I'll grant it, but a two-hander where 90% of the runtime is Marcos "listening" to a waitress monologue is not, actually, a film. It is a short story someone pointed a camera at.

You're very funny on your website. That's part of the problem. The website is doing work the films should be doing. If you spent half as much time on narrative scaffolding as you do on your "Standards" paragraph, you might, actually, make a final.

Respectfully, keep trying.

D. Reeves
Director, "Last Light at Echo Park" (2023 48 Hr Finalist)
Director, "Still Burn" (2025 48 Hr Finalist)
via YouTube comment

FHBP

Dear D.,

We looked up Last Light at Echo Park. Fine work. Nobody's seen it.

The trophy is on your mantelpiece. Our films are on YouTube. One of these two things has viewers.

— SD

Letter 3

Concerned Writer (Méliès Representative)

Dear Fat Baby,

I am sorry you did not finish your Rubber Made movie. Which is crazy because how hard is it to copy a 1-min Frech film. P.S. L'Homme à la tête en caoutchouc was made in 1901 and should stay in 1901. P.S. Adding a podcaster, a counter, and a clown, does not justify the seven extra minuts you made me sit through.

Marco F.
São Paulo
via Letterboxd review

FHBP

Dear Marco,

We did finish the movie. It is eight minutes. Méliès's version is one. We call this progress.

P.S. It is Rubber Head, not Rubber Made. Rubbermaid is the plastic container brand that sells totes, which is exactly where you should put your ideas next time. You won't need a big one.

— SD

Letter 4

Concerned Writer (Marxist Reading Available)

Rubber Head is a late-capitalist allegory. The counter is capital accumulation. The clown behind the glass is the capitalist specter, feeding on the counting itself. The podcaster's button is the exhausted gesture of the neoliberal subject performing the labor of containment that holds the system together. Your website describes the film as "a man in a booth, hitting a button." That is either incompetent or deliberate. I intend to teach this film in my Horror Studies elective next semester and I would like to know which.

Alan Goss, MFA
Film and Media Studies, Brooklyn
via email, Google Doc attached

FHBP

Dear Alan,

The counter is a counter. The clown is a clown. If either of them is also capitalism, we didn't plan it, but we'll take the credit.

Teach the Marxist reading. It is going to be the reading whether we endorse it or not. Welcome to criticism.

— SD

Letter 5

Concerned Writer (Offside Observer)

Junk Haulers should be renamed to just Junk. You seem under the impression we didn't like your Junkers rom-com because we are, what, polite? The real issue was your horror-comedy was neither funny nor scary. FHBP

Dear Rob,

First, it's romcom. One word. The hyphen is a Canadian affectation. Second, rom-com means romantic comedy. The only horror film here is your understanding of basic genre.

On the hockey analogy: the puck was in Brazil the whole time. That is where we were trying to score.

— SD
PS: You have been made honorary Brazilian. Enjoy.

Letter 6

Concerned Writer (Vancouver Set Dept.)

Listen, I have been watching your films from up here in Vancouver and I have to compliment your director on the set design. The gray bedsheet in Unicorn Warrior was a bold choice. The same gray bedsheet in Lost and Found was a bolder choice. Then I watched Junk Haulers and there was no sheet at all, which honestly broke me. I was waiting for the sheet. The sheet IS the visual language. Also, serious question out of professional respect: what does your director's actual bed look like at this point? He is clearly sleeping on the raw mattress and that is a level of commitment the rest of us would not make.

Kyle M.
Vancouver, BC
via Reddit DM

FHBP

Dear Kyle,

The director sleeps directly on the box spring now. The duvet became the set in a film we have not released. He finds the whole thing clarifying.

On Junk Haulers: the truck is the sheet, metaphorically. You are the first critic on the continent to catch this. You have been promoted to Associate Set Decorator effective immediately. Vancouver office, remote.

— SD
